# How tinue gets its puzzles

tinue is a free, Lichess-style puzzle trainer for Tak. Every puzzle on it is generated by us from
**PlayTak's public games database**; nothing is copied from another puzzle collection.

## The source

- **What:** `https://playtak.com/games_anon.db.gz`, the anonymised games dump that PlayTak links from
  playtak.com/games and regenerates daily (USTA's `playtak-api` serves it). `scripts/data-stats.mjs --raw
  data/raw/games_anon.db` prints how many games our copy holds.
- **What it contains:** each game's moves, result, board size, komi, time control, date, player
  usernames and ratings. No accounts, emails, passwords or chat; those aren't in the dump.
- **What we use:** moves and result to find puzzles. Usernames, ratings and game id are kept only as
  source credit ("SwedishChef vs ElirBot · game"), with a link to that game's PTN on PlayTak's own API.

## Which games

Rated games only, both players registered (no guests), standard piece counts, standard swap opening,
board sizes 4×4–8×8, most recent first. The first miner (`mine-playtak.mjs`) and the deep miner
(`mine-deep.mjs`) take **road wins**; the turning-point miner (`mine-swing.mjs`) also scans flat wins,
resignations, timeouts and draws.

For live counts (puzzles by size and length, by miner, the served pool, grades, attempts) run
`node --no-warnings scripts/data-stats.mjs [path/to/tinue.db]`. Counts are not kept in this file: they went stale.

## How a game becomes a puzzle

1. **Replay** the game with our own rules engine (checked against the standard move-count tables and
   ~6 million randomized consistency checks).
2. **Walk back from the winning move.** Look at the positions where the eventual winner was to move,
   1, 3 and 5 plies before the end (7 to 15 for the deep miner). Prove, by exhaustive search, whether each one is already a forced
   road win (a *tinuë*): a win no matter what the opponent does.
3. **Take the earliest such position:** the moment the tinuë appeared, usually right after the loser's
   mistake. That mistake is replayed as the puzzle's opening move, and the whole game before it is
   viewable, so you can see how the position arose.
4. **Build the solution tree:** *every* move that still forces the win is accepted (Tak positions often
   have several), and for each one the solver picks the opponent's most stubborn reply: the one that
   survives longest and leaves the fewest winning options. The real game's moves are preferred when
   they're equally good.
5. **Tag and rate:** themes (double threat, spread, smash, quiet move, …) are detected from the solution
   line. A starting rating comes from length (≈900 / 1500 / 2100 for 1 / 2 / 3 moves) adjusted for how
   many winning moves exist, whether a stack move is needed, board size, and how hard the search
   was. Ratings then adjust as players solve or fail each puzzle (Glicko-2, as on Lichess).
6. **Deeper puzzles** (tinuë in 4+ moves) use a proof-number prover, cross-checked against brute force.

Mining runs offline. Each puzzle ships with its precomputed answer tree, and the board is PTN Ninja's, embedded
the way PlayTak embeds it. The site does search in one case: a move outside the tree is checked
live (in the browser, or by an engine on the server) to see whether it still forces the win. The server
stores usernames, ratings and which puzzles you've tried.

## Other collections we looked at (and don't publish)

- **Ditaktic daily puzzles** by Nqeron (ditaktic.blogspot.com, ~1,050 hand-picked and annotated,
  plus a paid book, *Tak Puzzle Compendium*). It's a curated, copyrighted work. We used it privately
  only to check that our difficulty estimates rank puzzles the way a human puzzle author does. It is
  not on the site.
- **Jaffray-Ben/Tak-puzzles** (GitHub): ~10k 6×6 positions machine-mined from the same PlayTak dump.
  Licensed for local use only: we don't mine from it, and none of it is on the site.
- **puzzle.exegames.de** (nitzel): we only read a 36-puzzle sample to test our solver.

## If PlayTak wanted this

It's small: a JSONL export of puzzles (position, game id, solution tree, tags, rating), a small
zero-dependency server for ratings, and a page whose board is the PTN Ninja board PlayTak already uses. It could take
PlayTak accounts instead of its own usernames, and re-mine nightly from the daily dump.
